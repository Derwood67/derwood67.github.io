Characters

Authors show us who characters are through what they say, do, think, and feel. These are called character traits. Good readers also ask what each character wants and how the character changes.

Evan Treski

friendlyproudpeople-smart

Big brother, heading into fourth grade. He makes friends easily, but math is hard for him.

What he wants and how he changes

Evan wants to feel smart and respected. He starts out angry and hiding his feelings. By the end he admits what he did wrong and tells Jessie the truth.

Jessie Treski

math whizdeterminedpuzzled by feelings

Little sister. She is skipping third grade and will be in Evan's class. Numbers are easy for her, but people confuse her.

What she wants and how she changes

Jessie wants her brother back and wants to fit in at school. She learns to notice how others feel and to say sorry.

Megan Moriarty

kindgenerous

A girl from Evan's grade who becomes Jessie's lemonade partner and her friend.

Why she matters

Megan shows Jessie that she can make a friend on her own, and that money can be used to help others.

Scott Spencer

competitivehard to trust

A classmate Evan teams up with early on. Evan never feels good about him.

Why he matters

When money goes missing at a friend's house, Evan suspects Scott. That loss pushes Evan to finally come clean.

Mrs. Treski

caringbusy

Evan and Jessie's mom. She works from home and can tell something is wrong between her kids.

Why she matters

She does not fix the fight for them. Evan and Jessie have to solve it themselves.

Setting

Setting is when and where a story happens. A strong setting does a job. It can cause problems, change the mood, or push characters to act.

When

The last five days of summer vacation, during a heat wave. The story ends on Labor Day weekend, right before school starts.

Where

The Treski house and the streets of their neighborhood, the town center, and friends' houses nearby.

Why it matters

Hot days mean thirsty customers. The first day of school is coming fast, so the clock is always ticking.

Practice

How does the setting shape the story?

Think and write

Imagine this story happened in January instead of August. What would have to change? Name at least two things.

Text structure

Text structure is how an author builds and organizes a book. Jacqueline Davies made some smart choices about how the chapters fit together.

Time order

Events happen in sequence over five days. The countdown builds suspense.

Two points of view

Chapters take turns following Evan and Jessie. We learn what each one thinks, even when they will not tell each other.

Business-word chapter titles

Each chapter is named for a business term and starts with its definition. The term hints at what will happen.

Cause and effect

One choice leads to the next. A hurtful word causes a bet. A mean trick causes a bigger one.

    Main idea

    The main idea is what the story is mostly about. Supporting details are smaller facts and events that help build it. A good test: could you tell a friend the whole story in one or two sentences? That is your main idea.

    • Main idea covers the whole book or a big part of it.
    • A detail is true, but it is only one small piece.
    Practice

    Main idea or detail?

    Think and write

    Write the main idea of the book in your own words. Then list three details that support it.

    Theme

    The theme is the lesson about life that the story teaches. It is bigger than this one book. You could use it in your own life.

    Topic

    One or two words. Example: honesty

    Plot event

    Something that happens to these characters. Example: Evan and Jessie make a bet.

    Theme

    A full-sentence lesson with no character names. Example: Hiding the truth makes problems grow.

    To find a theme, ask: What did the characters learn? How did they change? What caused the trouble, and what fixed it?

    Practice

    Topic, plot event, or theme?

    Think and write

    Choose one theme from this book. Describe a time in your own life, or in another book, when that lesson was true.
